Lethal police actions and French elections · spillovers

Where the ripple stops

A death at the hands of police raises turnout in the commune where it happens. Does it also move the communes next door? Scroll through four decades of events and three urban units to see where the effect travels and where it does not.

1980–2022
689 deaths, mainland France
Act I · Four decades

One dot for every death

Between 1980 and 2022, Basta! records 689 deaths in police interventions that can be placed in a commune of mainland France. Keep scrolling to run the clock.

Act I · Concentration

A third of them around Paris

210 of the 689 deaths (30%) happened in the Paris urban unit, which holds 17% of the population of mainland France.

Act II · The urban unit

411 communes, one centre

INSEE's unité urbaine groups communes into one continuous built-up area. The Paris unit has 411 of them; 215 are in the analysis sample (shaded).

The question: if a death happens in one commune, does turnout move in the others?

Act II · From the centre

A death in Paris reaches the suburbs

When the death happens in Paris itself, turnout in the other communes of the urban unit rises by +1.08 points at the next election (95% CI 0.62 to 1.53), even though nothing happened there.

Act II · From the edge

A death in the suburbs stays local

Exposure to a death in any other commune of the same urban unit shows no effect: −0.04 points (SE 0.16, all urban units pooled). What travels is a death in the centre.

Act II · Lyon

The same event in Lyon goes nowhere

Lyon is the second-largest urban unit. Outside Paris, a death in the central commune moves turnout in its neighbours by −0.10 points (95% CI −0.45 to 0.25). No detectable spillover.

Act III · The estimates

Spillover is a Paris story

Direct effects look similar inside and outside Paris. Spillover appears only when the death happens in Paris and the neighbour belongs to its urban unit. Adding a 20 km distance ring barely changes the centrality estimate (0.58 to 0.51), so this is not proximity alone.

One reading is that national media treat Paris events as national news. The design cannot separate this from everything else that makes Paris unique.

Estimates: two-way fixed effects (commune and election), urban sample, first-order elections, 12-month window; standard errors clustered by commune. Spillover exposure means a death in the central commune of the same unité urbaine while the focal commune had none itself. Data: Basta! (basta.media), INSEE urban units (2020), commune boundaries from OpenStreetMap (2022). Each dot sits at the centre of its commune; no information about individual victims is shown.
